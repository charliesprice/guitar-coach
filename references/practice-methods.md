# Practice methods

Research-grounded methods the coach and the app use. When you recommend something, name the method so the user learns the "why".

## Deliberate practice (Ericsson et al.)
Improvement comes from focused work on specific weaknesses with immediate feedback, just beyond current ability — not from replaying what's comfortable. **Applied:** every plan has a Stretch block and a Skill-focus block; Polish items target the weakest spot in a song, not the whole song.

## Slow practice + metronome ladder
Accuracy first, then speed. Start ~60–70% of target, raise 2–4 BPM after 3 consecutive clean reps, drop back 5–10 BPM when it gets sloppy. Log the fastest *clean* BPM, not the fastest attempt. **Applied:** "Up to tempo" task text; BPM history per song.

## Weak-spot isolation and "stitching"
The 80% problem is usually transitions: each section is practised from its start, so the joins get the fewest reps. Loop the last bar of section A + first bar of section B. **Applied:** Stitching-stage task text.

## Backward chaining
Learn and rehearse the ending first, then prepend earlier sections, so the end of a piece becomes the most-practised part. **Applied:** Learning-stage task text alternates forward/backward.

## Practice mode vs performance mode
Practice mode stops and fixes. Performance mode never stops, whatever happens — recovery is its own skill. Most learners only do the first, so they can't play songs through. Gerald Klickstein's *The Musician's Way* treats performance preparation and recovery as things to practise deliberately. **Applied:** every Up-to-tempo and Maintenance item ends with one non-stop run; Performance-ready = a clean take recorded to a click.

## Interleaving (contextual interference)
Mixing several items in one session feels harder but transfers and lasts better than one long block on one thing (Shea & Morgan; studies in motor learning and music). **Applied:** blocks of ~10–15 min; no song more than ~25% of a 60+ min session.

## Spacing and spaced review
Revisiting material at growing intervals beats daily repetition for retention. **Applied:** Maintenance songs come back on a doubling interval (2 → 4 → 8 → 16 → 30 days) when clean (4+/5); reset to 2 days when shaky.

## Retrieval and mental practice
Playing a passage from memory, and rehearsing it mentally away from the guitar (hearing it, imagining fingerings), strengthen recall. Useful for days with little time.

## Recording and self-assessment
Recording reveals timing drift and tone problems you don't hear while playing. Doubles as the user's songwriting skill (recording clean parts to a click in Logic).

## Ear training through transcription
Working parts out by ear before checking tab builds relative pitch and fretboard knowledge together. Start with bass notes and simple riffs; check against tab after.

## Rhythm and groove
Strumming hand keeps moving (down on beats, up on "&"), whether or not it hits strings. Metronome on 2 and 4 to feel the backbeat. Muted "ghost" strums build 16th-note feel.
